Artists who build the machines they think with.

Machines We Imagine is an official UAL Group that supports research led by artists who build computational systems while making art. We also study how those tools, in turn, can change creative practice, culture, and society.

We hold that the creation of software, circuit board, an interface, a model, or a dataset entails arguments, ideas, and proposals. It is at this intersection that we explore. When code is treated as a material, it can shape the cognition of the artist who develops the technology, the people who encounter the art, use the software, or extend it in their own practices or companies. Where the dominant model of tech equates scale with success, Machines We Imagine holds that a more interpretive, reflective, and subtle process is at play. Artists are proposing possible future directions.

Remit: Three commitments 02

The creation of software by artists warrants more research and support.

Machines We Imagine (MWI) works on the instruments of computational creative practices: model architectures, interfaces, datasets, controllers, and the governance frameworks that decide how each may be used.

We study what a tool encodes about creative work, how it shapes the experience of the person using it, and how a tool built for exploration differs from one built for resolution in its outputs. We prioritize this as artist-led research.

We are artists who build tools.

Artists build tools as part of the art-making process, deliberately, and sometimes to make something easier for the next artist to come along. This can lead to thousands or millions of people learning something new, discovering a new possible form of expression, or nothing at all, except a single work of art. MWI gives that work a research home, technical infrastructure, and a route to funding.

Creative practice tool-building as the method.

Tools are developed through practice, tested with the practitioners who will use them, and assessed by their impact on work rather than by output-centric metrics alone. The studio's methods include autoethnography, participatory co-design, and relational, interpretive evaluation frameworks that explode and explore process.

Future key activities 03
i

Tool development

The studio designs and releases creative tools as concrete artifacts: prototype systems, open-source repositories, and documentation that enables independent practitioners to adopt them.

ii

Grant mentoring

“How do I fund this?” is often one of the questions artists ask themselves. As artists who have worked out how to fund, publish, and share their work, we aspire to make it easier for early-career researchers to do the same.

iii

Practice-led research and publication

Each tool is paired with a research output. The studio produces position papers, technical systems papers, and evaluation frameworks for venues across HCI, digital humanities, and creative AI, so that the knowledge generated by building a tool returns to the field and to the practitioners who informed it.

Funding small prototypes 04

Seed funding for prototypes that grow into grants.

We get a small amount of budget each year from UAL. We want to support the creation of prototypes and research that will generate grant funding and drive public engagement and knowledge exchange at UAL more broadly. If you're at UAL and want to join us, get in touch!

A Federated Data Commons and Governance Framework.
