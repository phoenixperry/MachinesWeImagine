Resident Thrum

An installation for group cardiac synchronisation through multimodal biofeedback.

Two to six people wear heart rate monitors and sit on a hexagonal grid of haptic tiles for a three-minute ritual. Their heartbeats drive vibration, sound and light, nudging the group towards synchrony.

At C&C '26

Groups of strangers sat the three-minute ritual together. A convener fitted the monitors, guided each session and led a short reflection afterwards.

How it works

Haptic

Each tile vibrates with its sitter's own heartbeat.

Sonic

Rhythms cohere and harmonies warm as sync rises.

Light

The central tile shifts from cool to warm tones.

Sync score

The range between the highest and lowest heart rate. Zero range means 100% sync.

From game to ritual

2018, Somerset House play test

The first version was a game: follow vibration cues to find hidden goals, inspired by the bee waggle dance. Most players ignored the goals. They sat or lay on the grid and attended to the vibrations themselves.

The work now centres synchronisation as both means and end.

Research questions

01

Does shared awareness of heart activity create a sense of a group body?

02

Does reported affect track measured synchrony?

03

Does making interoceptive signals conscious change how people understand social connection?

Method: exhibition-led design cycle (Smith and Dean's Iterative Cyclic Web). Each showing collects anonymised heart rate data plus a facilitated reflection after each session.

AHRC Braid funded / heldincommon.org

Held in Common

A federated data commons and governance framework for creative communities.

Sharing creative work and keeping control of it should not be opposites. Practitioners curate, control and share the datasets that train their tools, under conditions they define. A UK and US research collaboration.

First public output: the model landscape, a database of how open the "open" models really are.

01

Contribute

Your work lives on a local node you own and run.

02

Federate

Nodes share signals (patterns, summaries, agreements). Originals stay put.

03

Govern

The community sets the terms together, and anyone can withdraw at any time.

Meet Matthew

Digital Natives (2012) was a self-initiated project that started with an interest in 3D scanning using photogrammetry.

Co_former and CCC were tools I created in Processing to distort meshes and co-create algorithmic colour patinas, ready for print.

Digital Natives (2012) was acquired by the Pompidou in 2016, and has been exhibited in three shows as well as their permanent collection gallery.

Disarming Corruptor (2013) was a tool to circumvent online monitoring and evade copyright policing.

Disarming Corruptor won an Ars Electronica Award of Distinction in 2014, and was acquired by the Pompidou in 2016.
